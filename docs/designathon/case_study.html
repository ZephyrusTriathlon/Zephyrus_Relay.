Relay case studyProblem framing and scope
Problem framing

One network. Three brands. Too many disconnected decisions.

Waypoint Fresh, Style, and Tech share 60 vehicles, two depots, and limited daily capacity. Orders are re-entered into spreadsheets, plans depend on one dispatcher's memory, loading lists become stale, and proof of delivery is fragmented across paper and calls.

Relay creates one explainable record from order confirmation through receipt. It prioritizes feasibility, role-to-role handoffs, and recovery when normal operations break down.

High-fidelity scope

One complete Waypoint Fresh trip from Peliyagoda across all four roles, plus previews for Style and Tech. This slice makes the critical handoffs testable while the shared constraint model represents the wider network.

Source boundary

All records shown are synthetic. OUT001 to OUT120 and VEH001 to VEH060 follow the supplied dataset conventions. The confidential competition CSV files are not loaded into the browser.

Core tradeoff

Relay uses assisted planning with visible validation instead of a black-box auto-allocator. The dispatcher keeps control, while the system blocks infeasible weight, volume, refrigeration, access, depot, time, trip, fuel, and delivery-window choices.

Four user personas

Designed for where the work happens.

One Relay application serves four distinct people. Each account has a fixed role and opens only its relevant workspace. Shared delivery state connects their work. Waypoint assigns roles, so public signup and role self-selection are intentionally absent. Switch demo account is a judge convenience, not employee navigation.

Relay case studyNamed degradation scenario
Named degradation scenario

Connection lost during delivery.

The live example uses the Colombo route. Coverage loss is especially relevant in hill country, along the Kandy corridor and in rural districts. Field staff still need reliable carton checks, exceptions, arrivals, and receipts.

ConnectedRoute and last synchronization state are visible.
Connection lostThe interface identifies offline status and pending work.
Continue locallyLoading, exception, arrival, and receipt actions remain usable in the prototype.
Connection restoredThe simulated queue clears and confirms that updates are current.

Offline state

On a driver's phone or loader's tablet, safe actions remain usable and a pending-update count shows what awaits synchronization. Route changes stay protected, and outstanding stops remain visible.

Reconnected state

When coverage returns, queued updates reconcile in the simulation and the count clears, giving field staff a clear sign that their saved work is current.

Prototype boundary: offline work and synchronization are simulated with localStorage. Relay does not implement a service worker, backend queue, conflict resolution, or real cross-device synchronization.

Relay | Degradation
Relay case studyDecisions and prototype boundary
Scope, domain rules and decisions

Explainability before invisible automation.

Assisted planning

The dispatcher keeps control of the plan.

Visible checks block weight, volume, refrigeration, access, depot, time, trip, fuel, and delivery-window conflicts.

Prioritization

Fresh windows, chilled capacity, van-only access, and repeat deferrals create high service risk.

The interface puts those conditions beside the decision rather than hiding them in a separate report.

Reverse loading

The last delivery stop loads deepest.

The warehouse view numbers the manifest in loading order so unloading does not depend on a stale printout.

Shared lifecycle

One order moves through all four workspaces.

Each role sees the status and information needed for its next handoff.

Proof of delivery

Carton count, verification, recipient, and delivery time are explicit.

The Store manager can inspect the record before confirming receipt or reporting an issue.

Future capacity

A 10-week depot by brand view frames a staffing and fleet conversation.

The values are illustrative. Relay does not claim a submitted Datathon forecast or optimization engine.

Prototype boundary

This is a high-fidelity front-end prototype. Allocation, forecasts, synchronization, contacts, navigation, ETAs, and operational records are illustrative. State is stored locally in this browser. No live backend, real authentication, production infrastructure, or cross-device synchronization is claimed.

Style guide

One operational language across four roles.

Primary actions, feasible, complete

Amber

Attention, offline, timing risk

Operational surfaces

Shared patterns include 44 px minimum controls, consistent status language, visible focus, semantic headings, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts from 360 to 1440 px. Segoe UI and Arial keep the system font stack self-contained.
